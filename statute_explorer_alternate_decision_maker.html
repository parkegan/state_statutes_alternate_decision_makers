Definitions
Capacity
A patient is able to understand the nature and consequences of a decision, evaluate benefits, risks, and alternatives, and make and express a decision.
California § 4690: “Capacity” means a person’s ability to understand the nature and consequences of a decision and to make and communicate a decision, and includes in the case of proposed health care, the ability to understand its significant benefits, risks, and alternatives.
Competence
While technically referring to a judicial determination of mental status, is often used interchangeably with capacity in clinical and legal contexts.
Alabama § 22-8A-3: COMPETENT ADULT. An adult who is alert, capable of understanding a lay description of medical procedures and able to appreciate the consequences of providing, withholding, or withdrawing medical procedures.
Life-sustaining treatment (LST)
A medical treatment, procedure, or intervention that is necessary to sustain or restore vital functions. Depending on the state, may include the provision of artificial nutrition and hydration.
Nebraska § 20-403: Life-sustaining treatment shall mean any medical procedure or intervention that, when administered to a qualified patient, will serve only to prolong the process of dying or maintain the qualified patient in a persistent vegetative state.
Iowa § 144A.2: a. “Life-sustaining procedure” means any medical procedure, treatment, or intervention, including resuscitation, which meets both of the following requirements: (1) Utilizes mechanical or artificial means to sustain, restore, or supplant a spontaneous vital function. (2) When applied to a patient in a terminal condition, would serve only to prolong the dying process. b. “Life-sustaining procedure” does not include the provision of nutrition or hydration except when required to be provided parenterally or through intubation, or the administration of medication or performance of any medical procedure deemed necessary to provide comfort care or to alleviate pain.
